PayGuard — QA Fill Sheet (English)

Step 1 — Basic Info
Project Name: PayGuard
Industry: fintech
Current Stage: seed
One-line Description: A compliance assistant for SMBs that automates KYB/KYC checks, monitors transactions for fraud/AML risk, and generates audit-ready reports to reduce manual review time.

Step 2 — Value Proposition
1.1. Main customer problem: SMB fintechs and online businesses that handle payments (marketplaces, neobanks, PSP sub-merchants, crypto on-ramps, remittance, high-risk e-commerce) struggle with compliance. Manual reviews are slow and inconsistent, false positives waste time, and missing documentation creates audit risk. Teams can’t scale onboarding and monitoring without hiring expensive compliance analysts.
1.2. Your solution: PayGuard automates onboarding and ongoing monitoring: collects KYB/KYC data, runs sanctions/PEP/adverse media checks, scores risk, and monitors transactions for suspicious patterns. It provides case management workflows, SAR/STR drafting support, and produces audit-ready evidence packs and periodic compliance reports.
1.3. Uniqueness of your solution: PayGuard is designed for SMBs: fast setup, sensible defaults, and explainable risk scoring. We unify onboarding + monitoring + audit evidence in one place and support “human-in-the-loop” decisions with clear reasons, playbooks, and configurable rules—without needing a full enterprise GRC stack.
1.4. Key advantages over competitors: All-in-one onboarding + monitoring + audit packs; explainable risk decisions; quick integrations via API/webhooks; lower total cost than enterprise tools; strong case management with templates and role-based access.
1.5. Measurable value for customer: Reduce manual review time by 40–70%, cut onboarding time from days to minutes for low-risk customers, lower false positives by 20–35% via tuned rules, and improve audit readiness (fewer missing documents, faster evidence retrieval). Typical payback in 2–4 months by avoiding headcount growth.

Step 7 — Resources & Activities
6.1. Existing resources: Initial rules engine, case management UI, screening provider integrations (sanctions/PEP), and design partner relationships with access to anonymized historical alerts.
6.2. Resources needed: More data sources (adverse media, device/IP risk), robust audit evidence export, security controls (SSO, audit logs), and standardized integration connectors (webhooks/SDKs). Budget for pilots and compliance/legal templates.
6.3. Technology stack: TypeScript services, Postgres, Redis/queue, event streaming (Kafka/SQS), rule engine + scoring service, AWS hosting, SIEM/logging, and encryption at rest/in transit. Integrations via REST + webhooks.
6.4. Vendor dependencies: Dependence on third-party screening and data providers. Risk: price changes or coverage gaps. Mitigation: abstraction layer for multiple providers and caching/deduping checks to reduce costs.
6.5. Key Activities: Building and maintaining risk models and rules, monitoring system performance, onboarding customers (data mapping), maintaining compliance documentation and audit evidence workflows, and continuously tuning false positive rates.
6.6. Key Partners: Data providers (sanctions/PEP/adverse media), compliance consultancies (referrals), and integration partners (payment processors, KYC vendors).

Step 8 — Competition
7.1. Direct competitors: Enterprise monitoring tools (expensive, complex), mid-market AML platforms, and KYC vendors that are strong at onboarding but weaker in end-to-end monitoring + audit evidence.
7.2. Indirect competitors/alternatives: Manual reviews in spreadsheets, basic rule checks inside a payment processor dashboard, hiring more compliance analysts, or outsourcing monitoring to a consultancy.
7.3. Why choose you over competitors: PayGuard gives SMBs enterprise-grade workflows (cases, evidence, reporting) with fast implementation and explainable decisions at a price and complexity level that matches smaller teams.
7.4. Competitive defensibility: Defensibility through workflow lock-in (cases, evidence, policies), integrations, and continuous tuning based on customer feedback. Over time, anonymized patterns and rule libraries improve performance and reduce false positives.

Step 9 — Risks
8.1. Technical risks: Data quality and integration complexity (many payment events, edge cases) can create missed alerts or noisy output. Mitigation: staged rollout (shadow mode), robust testing with historical data, and clear monitoring/alerting of the monitoring system itself.
8.2. Financial risks: Sales cycles in regulated markets can be long, and data providers are costly. Mitigation: focus on design partners, standardize integration, price pilots appropriately, and secure annual prepay contracts to improve cash flow.
8.3. Legal/regulatory risks: Handling sensitive customer and transaction data creates GDPR and security obligations; wrong guidance must avoid being “legal advice.” Mitigation: strong data controls, DPAs, security reviews, and positioning as a tooling platform with configurable policies (customer remains responsible for reporting).
8.4. Market risks: Incumbents may bundle monitoring features, and buyers may be cautious about switching. Mitigation: prove ROI quickly, integrate with existing KYC/processor tools, and offer migration support plus audit packs.
8.5. Team risks: High domain complexity requires experienced compliance talent; hiring can be slow. Mitigation: advisors + part-time MLRO support early, documented playbooks, and hiring a senior compliance engineer after product fit.
8.6. Risk mitigation plan: Next 90 days: 1) ship audit log + evidence export v1, 2) add a second screening provider as fallback, 3) complete 2 pilots with measured false-positive reduction, 4) finalize security baseline (SSO, RBAC), 5) publish trust center and standard DPA.
